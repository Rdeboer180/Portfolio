# Mentor lenses and evidence

Use this reference to choose standards, not to imitate personal voice. Public material was reviewed July 21, 2026. Treat time-sensitive roles and opinions as research that may require refreshing.

## Shared recognition bar

Across the seven leaders, strong work tends to make five things visible:

1. **A point of view:** taste is selection and judgment, not surface polish.
2. **Working evidence:** prototypes, shipped systems, code, motion, or tools outperform labels such as “AI-native.”
3. **Craft after generation:** first output is raw material; differentiation appears in iteration, precision, and restraint.
4. **Systems and consequences:** show how a choice behaves across real states and moves toward production.
5. **Authorship with agency:** AI expands exploration, but the designer remains responsible for intent, decisions, care, and outcomes.

## Jakub Krehel — encode judgment into the workflow

**Observed focus**

- Founding design engineer; publishes *Interfaces* and agent skills for UI, typography, color, layout, and motion.
- Frames skills as a way to encode judgment and understanding into agents.
- Treats typography, animation, and “small but important details” as operational product quality.
- Signals respect for people who refuse to sacrifice either form or function.

**What he is likely to recognize**

- A precise interface principle demonstrated in a working artifact.
- Reusable design intelligence: a token, component behavior, skill, constraint, or evaluation loop.
- A before/after whose improvement is explained, not merely shown.
- Design engineering where code gives the designer finer control.

**Post test**

> What judgment did you encode so the next interface or agent output becomes better by default?

## Loredana Crisan — taste is care; AI broadens exploration

**Observed focus**

- Figma Chief Design Officer leading product design, research, writing, and brand.
- Defines taste as accumulated intentional choices and ultimately as care for user, craft, and result.
- Argues that AI can broaden exploration but cannot substitute for vision or accepting responsibility for the final result.
- Describes AI experience design as curating responsive systems and datasets rather than drawing only a happy path.
- Values creative agency, cross-disciplinary intersections, iteration, and removing friction around productive mess rather than eliminating the mess itself.

**What she is likely to recognize**

- A designer who uses AI for breadth, then molds the final 30% with precision.
- Systems designed for variable intent, edge cases, and human agency.
- Craft framed as strategic advantage and care, not decoration.
- Work spanning design, product, engineering, research, or another creative discipline.

**Post test**

> Where is the care, and which intentional choices make the result unmistakably yours after AI has generated the possibilities?

## Kirk McNeill — foundations before automation; adoption over perfection

**Observed focus**

- Founder of UI Collective and a self-taught product designer whose teaching centers on practical UI craft, design systems, tokens, variables, component construction, and career-ready working knowledge.
- Treats design systems as products that succeed through adoption, shared structure, governance, documentation, and collaboration with developers rather than component volume or theoretical perfection.
- Argues that designers need to understand variables, tokens, components, and design standards before AI can extend their work responsibly.
- Experiments publicly with Figma, MCP, Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, and design-system mirroring while staying candid about current limits. He has described Figma-to-code workflows as promising but not yet automatically production-ready when semantic alignment, local components, or system documentation are missing.
- Recommends breadth across evolving AI tools, but treats the tool as secondary to the designer's ability to audit output and understand the system it is acting on.

**What he is likely to recognize**

- A real design system used by designers and developers, with evidence of adoption, maintenance, or implementation rather than a pristine Figma library alone.
- A concrete token, variable, component, responsive, or documentation decision that made the system easier for people or agents to consume.
- An AI workflow grounded in existing design context, followed by a clear account of what drifted, what Ryan corrected, and why the result was or was not ready for production.
- Honest tool comparison based on building the same artifact or workflow, not speculative rankings or AI hype.
- Teaching that helps other designers understand the foundations underneath an automated workflow.

**Post test**

> Did the workflow make the system easier to adopt and extend, and can Ryan explain the UI or system judgment that AI could not supply on its own?

## Useful tensions to preserve

- **Story versus artifact:** Geoco and Riddering reward narrative; Choi often lets the prototype speak. Choose according to the strength of the evidence.
- **Systems versus rule-breaking:** Krehel, Crisan, and McNeill value systems; Tredly values designers who can bend them in pursuit of a better result. Explain the reason and consequence.
- **Speed versus care:** AI increases throughput, but none of the seven treats raw speed as the outcome. Show what additional exploration or refinement the speed enabled.
- **Automation versus foundations:** McNeill treats tokens, variables, components, and standards as prerequisites for useful AI work. Show what system context the agent received and what judgment Ryan still had to apply.
- **Public polish versus candor:** Professional clarity matters, but candid stakes and real uncertainty can carry more credibility than a polished victory lap.
- **AI fluency versus AI branding:** Demonstrate AI through artifacts, decisions, and workflow. Do not use the label as a substitute for evidence.
