# Portfolio audit — August 2026

Five independent audits, run in parallel, consolidated and spot-checked.

**Lenses:** `ryan-design-taste` (does the site embody the taste it sells) · `impeccable` (interface craft) · `accessibility` (WCAG 2.2) · `case-study-layout` (7-section narrative) · docs-vs-code (a sweep the taste audit spawned itself).

**Verification:** every claim below was re-checked by hand against source, compiled CSS, or `build/` output. Contrast ratios were recomputed from the WCAG relative-luminance formula, not trusted from the reports. Anything a code audit *cannot* confirm is listed under "Needs a browser pass" and is not stated as fact.

## The one thing to decide before anything else

**`/design-system` renders fabricated testimonials and a Walmart-branded case study.**

- `DesignSystem.tsx:476-488` — two testimonials attributed to **named individuals** ("Paul Bennett, Founder"; "Fawn Rolands, Sr. Product Manager, Acme Corp"), five stars each, with specific quotes praising Ryan.
- `DesignSystem.tsx:543-564` — a card branded **"Walmart"** ("Empowering item discovery with Gen AI summaries") carrying metrics `+1.1% ATC Conversion` and `75% Found Helpful`; plus a **"DemocracyOS"** card with `role="Product Designer & Founder"`, `year="2025"`.

The route is `noindex` and excluded from the sitemap — but it is a live public URL, and nothing on the page marks any of this as placeholder.

`PRODUCT.md:45` states: *"Real metrics and quotes only. Future work must **never** fabricate numbers, testimonials, or endorsements, and must keep every quote attributable."*

This is not a design defect. A Walmart case study with conversion metrics reads as employment history, and named five-star testimonials read as endorsements from real people. **Left untouched pending Ryan's answer** — the fix depends on which, if any, of it is real.

## Where the audits converged

Independent agents reaching the same root cause from different directions is the strongest signal in the set. Three did.

### 1. The brand orange fails AA as text — found by a11y *and* craft, independently

Recomputed from source:

| Pair | Ratio | AA 4.5:1 |
|---|---|---|
| `#f03d01` on `#ffffff` | **3.91:1** | fail |
| `#f03d01` on `#f5f5f5` | **3.58:1** | fail |
| `#f03d01` on `#fdede9` (SectionBadge eyebrow) | **3.44:1** | fail |
| white on `#f03d01` (`.btn--primary` **at rest**) | **3.91:1** | fail |
| `#fdede9` on `#c23001` (`.btn--primary` **hover**) | 4.97:1 | pass |
| `$color-primary-dark` `#c23001` on `#ffffff` | **5.65:1** | pass |

Two things make this worse than a normal contrast bug. `_base.scss:88-90` also strips underlines from every link, so orange link text fails **1.4.3 and 1.4.1 together** — colour becomes the only link cue. And the primary CTA passes *only on hover*, which is backwards.

The fix already exists in the token file. This wants a new **role** token (`--color-link` / `--color-primary-text` → `#c23001`) rather than darkening the brand swatch globally: `#f03d01` stays valid for fills, rules, and large display type, where 3.91:1 clears the 3:1 non-text bar.

### 2. `/design-system` publishes contrast numbers that are wrong

All four cards recomputed:

| Card | Published | Actual | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary on White | `4.6:1 ✓` | **3.91:1** | fails, marked passing |
| Muted on White | `4.5:1 ✓` | **4.29:1** | fails, marked passing |
| Dark on White | `13.5:1 ✓` | 17.22:1 | mislabeled (it is white-on-dark) |
| Neutral on White | `7.2:1 ✓` | 8.86:1 | understated |

The "Muted" card displays `#7a7a7a`, which **is not the token**. The real value is `#707070` (`_tokens.scss:20`), which *passes* at 4.95:1 — and its own comment asserts AA compliance. The page invents a worse colour than the site ships, then certifies it. The wrong hex is repeated at `DesignSystem.tsx:30, 287, 387, 426, 508, 579` and `tailwind.config.js:22`.

This is the page a design leader opens to test whether the systems claim is real. Two wrong numbers here cost more than the underlying contrast bug.

### 3. `ownership` is declared and orphaned — found by case-study *and* taste, independently

`projects.ts:119` declares `ownership?: string[]`. **0 of 11** projects populate it. The only code that reads it (`CaseStudyPage.tsx:864, 937`) sits inside the legacy branch that `hasNewFormat` (`:493`) never reaches — it is `true` for all 11 projects. `takeaways` is in the same state.

So the field for "what I decided vs what I was handed" exists, is unreachable, and attribution survives only as prose buried mid-paragraph (`projects.ts:165`, `:849`).

## Where the audits genuinely disagreed

Kept rather than flattened, because the disagreement is informative.

**Images: "under-illustrated" vs "restrained."** The case-study audit measured every section against the layout skill's per-section image counts and reported widespread under-illustration — Constraints has images on 0 of 11, Problem on 2 of 11. The craft audit never raised image volume; it raised image *dimensions* (27 of 29 `<img>` ship without `width`/`height`, guaranteeing CLS).

**Resolution: the counts in that skill are ceilings, not quotas.** Zero images in a Problem section is not automatically a defect, and 46/46 images being captioned is evidence of restraint working. The real finding underneath is narrower and worth acting on: `landing-pages` (`projects.ts:720-784`) has **0 images in Approach and 4 in Outcome** — the one place imagery pads a result instead of proving process. Fix that one; leave the rest.

**The hero.** The taste audit judged the intro overlong against a 3–4s budget. The craft audit measured it at **~10.3s** to a resolved headline and went further: the `<h1>` is visually hidden and contains four subject-less aphorisms, while the actual positioning sits in a 12px `<p class="hero__eyebrow">`. The craft framing is the stronger one — this is a semantics problem, not a duration problem — but both point the same way.

## Critical — verified

**C1 · Hydration mismatch discards the prerender on every route.**
`index.tsx:17-21` · `UnlockContext.tsx:57-68` · `SiteUnlockBar.tsx:59`
Confirmed against build output: every prerendered file contains `.unlock-bar` **and** `<html class="has-unlock-bar">`. On first client render `ready` is `false`, so the bar returns `null` — a structural mismatch at the root. React 19 discards the tree and re-renders client-side, and the nav shifts 40px before settling. The comment at `UnlockContext.tsx:53-55` asserts the prerender captures neither bar nor modal; the build output disproves it.

**C2 · The prerendered homepage is frozen mid-animation.**
Confirmed: `build/index.html` carries `data-intro-stage="sketch"` — stage 2 of 8. With JS off or slow, the first paint is construction crosshairs, sketch circles, and a pencil placeholder where the portrait goes. The real `<h1>` is clipped to `rect(0,0,0,0)`, so there is no visible headline either.
Fix in `prerender.mjs`: run the Playwright context with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (the reduced-motion path at `Hero.tsx:115-118` jumps straight to `done`), or `waitForSelector('[data-intro-stage="done"]')`.

**C3 · Signal Orange fails AA as text.** See convergence §1.

**C4 · The unlock bar covers the navigation on `/notes`, `/notes/:slug`, `/resume`, `/sitemap`.**
Confirmed: `_unlock-bar.scss` offsets exactly three navs. Grep for `notes__nav`, `resume-page__nav`, `sitemap-page__nav` in that file returns **0** each. Those navs are `sticky; top: 0; z-index: 1020` against the bar's `1030` — so once scrolled, they pin underneath it and disappear. Those pages' only navigation is gone, for every visitor who hasn't entered a password.

**C5 · Collapsed accordion panels keep links in the tab order.**
`FAQ.tsx:98-105` + `_faq.scss:171-181`. Collapsed with `max-height: 0; overflow: hidden` only — no `hidden`, no `inert`. Five links inside stay focusable and announced; keyboard focus lands in a zero-height box. The correct pattern already ships at `CandidateSnapshot.tsx:78`. The same rules also hard-cap expanded answers at `600px`, silently clipping the two longest.

**C6 · `/design-system` publishes false pass marks.** See convergence §2.

**C7 · A global `text-transform: capitalize` rewrites authored copy.**
`_base.scss:52` applies it to every `h1`–`h6`, with only 5 resets site-wide — and Notes body headings are not among them. Essay titles render Title Cased against what was written. Notes is where the writing *is* the product.

## Runtime evidence — reproduced independently

A sixth pass ran the built site in a real browser. Two findings could not have been caught by reading source, and I reproduced both myself before recording them.

### R1 · The doubled-render bug — most severe finding in the audit

On **any** URL that falls back to `build/index.html` — `/work`, `/work/tire-rack-winter`, `/work/photography-workflow-agent`, and every unknown URL — hydration bails and React **appends a second full copy of the page instead of replacing the first**.

Reproduced on a static host with SPA fallback, `/totally-bogus`:

```
#root children: 6 → [DIV.unlock-bar, A.skip-link, MAIN, DIV.unlock-bar, A.skip-link, MAIN]
<h1>: 2   <main>: 2   <nav>: 2   headings: 68 (vs 34)   document height: 32,876px
main #1 at y=56   ·   main #2 at y=16,466
```

**The visible copy is the dead one.** Copy #1 — the one at the top of the page — is stuck on the first animation frame: headline reads **"Fluent in tools"**, and the portrait never resolves. Copy #2, the live React tree, has settled correctly to "Care for what ships" but sits 16,000px down where nobody will scroll. Screenshot confirms a grey placeholder ring with a generic person outline where the portrait belongs.

The address bar reads `/` after `<Navigate>` fires, so **nothing signals to the visitor that anything went wrong** — they simply see a broken hero and a page twice as long as it should be.

This is the same root cause as C1 (the prerendered unlock bar), escalated: on a matched route React recovers by re-rendering; on a fallback route it duplicates. Fixing C1 in `prerender.mjs` fixes this too.

### R2 · `/design-system` ships the homepage's title and canonical

Confirmed in the built file: `<title>Ryan DeBoer | Product Design Engineer · Systems · Front-End</title>` and `<link rel="canonical" href="https://www.rdeboerdesigns.com/">` — pointing at the homepage, not itself. Every other route is correct (`/about/` → `/about/`). Cause: `DesignSystem.tsx` is the only page component that never calls `usePageMeta`.

There is also **no `build/404.html`**; `App.tsx:145` routes every unknown URL to the homepage, so bad URLs return HTTP 200 and a soft-404.

### R3 · Measured layout breaks

| Width | What breaks |
|---|---|
| **320** | `/resume` header overflows +97px (three elements clipped); homepage testimonials stack +16px |
| **375** | `/resume` header +42px — "Back to Portfolio" renders 14px of 55px and is **unreachable**, because `_base.scss:22` sets `overflow-x: clip` on `html, body` so there is no scrollbar to recover it. `/design-system` swatch names clip mid-string (`--color-primary-`). The `#projects` anchor lands 8px under the fixed nav. |
| **480 / 640** | `/resume` accent divider bleeds ±48px past the card (`_resume-page.scss:176-188`; the responsive blocks never reset it) |
| **768+** | No measured overflow on any route |

`/resume` at 375 is the worst visible break on the site: the logo wraps into the "Save contact" button, "Save as PDF" wraps to three lines inside its pill, and the back link is sliced by the viewport edge.

### R4 · Weight

`build/images` is **205 MB across 225 files** — largest singles 10.5 MB, 9.0 MB, 6.7 MB, and many duplicated byte-for-byte between `build/images/work/<slug>/` and `build/assets/portfolio-safe/<slug>/`. The homepage pulls 2,265 KB over 46 requests, including a 546 KB JS bundle and a 203 KB stylesheet.

Also measured: **54 of 55 homepage `<img>` ship without `width`/`height` and without `loading="lazy"`**.

### What the runtime pass cleared

Two things the static audits flagged as suspicious turned out to be **fine**, and are withdrawn:

- **Focus rings work.** All six sampled controls returned `solid 2px rgb(240,61,1)` with a 2px offset, and the first 14 tab stops at 375 all matched `:focus-visible` with no trap and nothing skipped. The `outline: none` rules are correctly out-specified.
- **The mobile nav is escapable** — `aria-expanded` toggles correctly, Escape closes it, focus returns to the toggle. It does still lack a scroll lock (the page scrolls behind it), but there is no trap.

The proficiency dock's roving tabindex is also correct: one tab stop, not nine.
