# Binder specimen: twelve studies, one light

19–20 October 2026. Fable's pass in the Astra → Fable → Opus sequence for the collectible binder. All twelve case studies sit in the binder with the hero art Ryan chose, under a holographic finish tinted from each hero and lit by one lamp. Hover lifts a card 10% out of its sleeve. A click flips a card on its way to the case-study panel, where it tucks into a sleeve of its own at the top of the study; a protected card goes once the site's shared password has opened it. The binder's spine files the work by discipline, and Next and Previous turn the page on its rings. Exploration only: nothing here touches `src/`, the published order, project facts, or the live site.

## Run it

From the repo root:

```bash
node docs/explorations/binder-specimen/build-cards.mjs
```

```bash
python3 -m http.server 3100 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Then open <http://127.0.0.1:3100/docs/explorations/binder-specimen/>. The `.claude/launch.json` entry `binder-specimen` runs the same server. Intro videos and posters load from `public/`, so serve the repo root, not this folder. Python's server doesn't answer Range requests, so there an intro video can't seek to its poster's frame and starts from its first; any server that does (nginx on the live site) shows the handoff as built.

Until 21 October (now parked, see above), `share-build.mjs` wrote `share/`: the binder without the bench or the review notes, with a short intro, the card data inlined, and the heroes and intro videos beside the page. `share/index.html` is a page body for Claude's Artifact publisher, which adds the doctype and head; `share/preview.html` wraps it the same way for checking locally. `--blur-locked` blurs the art on locked cards. On 20 October it was published at Ryan's request, with the locked art as built, as a private Artifact for him to share in his Discord for feedback; republish to the same Artifact to keep the link. The publisher doesn't serve `.vtt`, so the shared page leaves PlayDraft's captions track out.

`build-cards.mjs` reads `src/data/projects.ts`, `src/data/playdraft.json` and the hero manifest, writes `cards.js`, and puts each card's hero in `heroes/`: the newest `artwork/` PNG for that card, saved as a JPEG through macOS `sips`. In a worktree without `plans/new`, pass the hero folder as the first argument. `capture.mjs` clears `captures/`, renders every still and three screen recordings with the repo's Playwright while the server runs, and prints the behaviour checks. It launches Chromium with GPU flags, because software rendering halves the frame rate of the moving light and paints the heroes late in the recordings. It turns the recordings into MP4 with `ffmpeg` when it is installed and keeps WebM otherwise.

## What the page is

A gray binder page in work order: six pockets per page on desktop, four on a tablet, three on a phone, with a pager under it whose Next and Previous turn the page. Every pocket has the same four layers, back to front: the backing well, the card's shadow, the card, the sleeve.

**The spine.** On desktop (1180px and up) the binder has a cover spine on its left, made like a collector's binder: a padded leatherette with a pebble grain (an SVG lighting filter, laid on in soft light), rounded over its outer edge and dipping into the hinge, with contrast stitching inset from every edge. The RDB monogram is stamped in silver foil and pressed in. Under it is the work, filed the way the site files it, as tabs that press into the leather; the chosen one carries a foil bar: All work (12), Product Design (5), System Design (6) and AEM (4). WheelRack, a full design-system build, is filed under System Design as well as Product Design at Ryan's word, so it leads that tab too. A tab filters the binder to those studies in work order. A page that runs short keeps its pockets and shows the rest as empty sleeves, the way a real binder page would. Picking a card outside the tab returns to All work. Below desktop the same tabs run along the cover's top edge with the monogram, on the same stitched leatherette, and on a phone they shorten to All, Product, Systems and AEM. Down the left, the cover shows as a narrow edge (18px on a tablet, 14px on a phone) with the ring plate on its seam, as on desktop, so the holes are punched in the page's margin between the binder's edge and the sleeves, and the light keeps off the edge. Heatherwood has no discipline, so it appears only under All work.

**The rings.** The mechanism is a domed, brushed-steel plate down the spine's edge, riveted at both ends. Each ring rises out of a collar on it, arcs over in two halves that meet at a visible seam, drops through a punched hole with a clear reinforcing washer, and casts a soft shadow on the page; the hole's depth swallows the end of the wire. The wire is drawn as a chrome tube: a dark silhouette, the steel's reflections along it, and across it a lit crown, the horizon, a shaded underside and a rim of light bounced off the page. The steel catches the page's one light: a glint slides along the plate at the lamp's height (`--ly` on `.rings`), and each ring brightens as the lamp comes level with it (`--glint`). Below desktop the plate sits on the seam of the cover's narrow edge.

**One light.** A lamp hangs over the middle of the binder, and sheets lie over every pocket at once, one blanket over all the cards. `.binder__light` dims the page with distance from the lamp. Three more slide with the lamp's reflection: `.binder__band` is a pale band of light, `.binder__prism` the rainbow the foil throws round the reflection, and `.binder__gloss` the glare on the plastic. The three sit inside one `.binder__glare` sheet, and only that sheet blends with the page (screen): one blended layer the size of the binder instead of three. Ryan took the cost on 21 October: the band is a few percent dimmer where it crosses the reflection, and the rainbow screens at 20% rather than soft-lighting at 53%, so it lifts the foil's colour a little instead of deepening it. The glare and the band fall on the sleeves only, at 70% (Ryan, 20 October), the rainbow on the cards' foil only, and the spine gets none of it. The reflection keeps its own slow sway, at 70% of its first speed, easing toward where it is going over 600ms rather than 420; the pointer only tilts the page a little, so the glare drifts away from it without being pinned to it. The pointer's other effect is a hand: a wide, faint shadow cast away from the lamp, which dims the glare when it passes over it. It darkens the page by about 9% at its centre (it was 22% until Ryan found it too strong) and trails the pointer slightly. Each card's own finish (the tinted metal, the rainbow film and its streaks, the glitter, the foil ring) is laid in binder space and never moves, so neighbouring cards continue one sheet of foil rather than repeat it.

**Type colours.** Each card's hue (`THEME`) tints a pale metal ground, under a rainbow film and a light glitter, with pale rows so the copy reads. On 20 October Fable tried stronger versions, with the ground up to three times as saturated and a band of the type colour across the header. Ryan kept this pastel finish.

**Turning a page.** Next and Previous turn the page on its rings, in 0.9 seconds. What turns is a copy of the page (its cards and their foil) in two segments, so the sheet can bow, and the page's own light lies over it as over the page: it lifts at its free edge, which leads on the way up and trails on the way down, swings over the rings, and comes down flat on the cover, under the ring plate and arcs, where it lies until a turn back lifts it. Until 25 October it fell away past the spine and faded as it went, 22° short of flat; Ryan asked for a turn where "the old page doesn't just vanish", saw the two side by side (`docs/reviews/binder-turn-2026-10-25/`) and chose the landing. Its shadow crosses the hinge onto the cover ahead of it, and lying there it is lit as the cover is, a little further from the lamp than the page. The sheet on the cover is the page before, so a click on it turns back. Below desktop the cover is out of view, so the sheet leaves past the binder's edge instead, with no fade in either direction. Every frame is worked out up front, so each half shows its back at the moment it passes the eye, not at 90 degrees, since the eye sits over the middle of the page. The back of the sheet is a page of empty sleeves, mirrored. The sheet shades as it turns from the lamp, the lamp's reflection slides off its plastic, and its shadow falls on the page under it, which is the real next page from the first frame. Previous runs the same turn backwards: the page before lifts off the cover, comes back over the rings and lands on a flat copy of the page you were on. A second click finishes the turn under way before starting the next. The light holds still while a page turns, and hover and clicks on the cards wait for it to land. Reduced motion changes the page at once.

On a touch screen a sideways drag across the cards turns the page under the finger (Ryan, 20 October): right to left lifts the page over the rings, left to right brings the one before back. The turn starts held at its first frame and the drag scrubs it. Let go past a third of the way, or with a flick, and the sheet finishes its turn; short of that it falls back and the page it left returns. A drag up or down still scrolls, and a drag never opens a card.

The turn was choppy on Ryan's phone, so it was measured (frame gaps, long animation frames and where in the swing each dropped frame fell, in Chromium on desktop and on a phone-sized page with the CPU slowed four times) and rebuilt around what the numbers showed. The turn animates only transform and opacity, so the compositor runs it; a face shows by opacity, stepped as it passes the eye, and one waiting its turn sits at 0.4% so it is painted before the swing, not in it. The real page changes before anything moves (going back, a flat copy of the old page covers it until the sheet lands), and the turn holds its first frame for 60ms, 140ms on a turn back or to a page never shown, while that painting happens. Flat is never quite flat (0.05 degrees), so the sheet is already a 3D layer when it starts. The copy carries no light sheets, whose blend modes and masks had been riding inside the 3D layers. Once the page is idle the other pages' art is loaded and decoded, and a turn is rehearsed out of sight, so a visitor's first turn has less to paint. Repeat turns now run without a dropped frame both ways; on the phone-sized page, first turns do too. On desktop, a first turn forward spends its one long frame in the hold, and the first turn back can still drop one frame early in the swing. Two things tried and dropped: laying the incoming page flat before a turn back made every turn back drop a frame, and `will-change` changed nothing. The landing (25 October) adds one compositor animation, the cover's shadow, and builds the flat sheet that stays on the cover in the frame after the swing, when nothing moves; measured the same way, it ran with no gap over 20ms in a quiet run where the fade had 33 to 50ms gaps inside its hold.

**Hover.** A pointer, pen or keyboard focus lifts a card 10% out of its sleeve (15% until Ryan asked for less on 20 October), and pulls it rather than slides it: the card tips back 4° about its bottom edge, its top coming toward you. The card in hand catches the lamp as you move: its holo film and glitter slide up to 7px against the pointer (`FOIL_SHIFT`), a transform on that one card. Both are from the Fable review of 20 October. The raised card rides over its neighbour's plastic and stays under its own sleeve's lip, and its shadow hardens into a crisp contact line and one cast that falls away from the lamp (`--sx`, `--sy`, set per pocket in `layout()`), longer for the tilt. The dark halo it used to carry went with the review: it darkened the card above, toward the lamp. Touch skips the hover; a tap opens the study. Where a raised card stands over the pocket above it (on a phone, and in a desktop's lower row), that pocket's glare and the card's rainbow keep off it, so it sits on top of that plastic, not under it: the light's masks get a hole where a raised card crosses another pocket, kept until the card is home.

**Opening a public card.** The panel's top comes in three versions, switched on the bench:

- **Card in a sleeve beside the video** (the default, Ryan's pick). The top of the study holds a pocket like the binder's, beside the intro video and the same height as it. The card tucks into the pocket, and the reel comes out from behind it as a printed insert: folded in three, it slides out on its card-stock back (the card's own pale metal), unfolds along its creases one panel at a time, each panel shading as it turns from the lamp and the insert's shadow widening on the page, and lies flat exactly where the video plays. Then the video takes over. On a phone the card flips over, then unfolds into the video (Ryan, 20 October, the third of three pitches, then made a real fold so nothing morphs). The card turns over in flight, slowly (a full second), and lands flush left on the video's slot, level with the panel's text, its back the left of the intro's first frame (or of its image). There a gate takes over on the same pixels, and the rest of the frame unrolls to the right as the desktop reel does: the first panel, folded behind the card, swings out on its left edge, card stock until it passes edge-on and then the print, then the second panel, folded behind the first. Both lie flat as the whole frame; then the video takes over on that frame. Closing, it rolls up right to left behind the card, the card takes over on its back and turns as it flies home. The card and the gate overlap for a few frames at each hand-off: swapped on the same instant, real Safari showed the gate a frame late and left one blank frame (Ryan, 20 October: flip slower, flush left with the text, unroll left to right like desktop; it had landed centred with flaps opening to both sides). Once open, the card is filed as a chip beside All work (its art and "Card 02"), which puts it back in the binder. Tried and dropped the same day: stacked, tucked behind the video, cased on a corner, bare on a corner, and the frame simply widening out of the card. Desktop and tablet keep the sleeve and the reel beside the video.
- **Card and intro, video below.** The pocket sits beside the study's context, which says who it was for, and its problem: the one-line `problemPunch` over the paragraphs from the full study's Problem section. The video follows at the full width of the column.
- **Card becomes the video** (last pass). The card's back is the intro video's first frame. It lands on the panel's video and unfolds into it.

Beside the video the open runs 2.2 seconds, so the insert has room to unfold; with the intro it runs 1.5. The card's part is the same in both:

| Time | What happens |

The video then plays, focus moves to Close, and the page behind is inert and stops scrolling. Close, Esc, the scrim or "All work" run it backwards (1.7 seconds beside the video, 1.25 with the intro): the insert folds up and slides behind the card, or the text fades; the card slides up out of the pocket, turns back on its way home, the panel slides out, and the card drops into its binder sleeve. Focus returns to the card. After Esc the card stays raised with its focus ring; after a pointer close it settles.

**Locked cards** lift on hover and carry a lock on the art window. A click opens the password prompt in the site's own two steps: Unlock (primary) or Keep it in the sleeve (secondary), then a password field with Unlock and Back. The prompt takes the site's shareable password (the one the résumé page prints), checked as the site checks it, by SHA-256 against the hash the site's bundle carries (`build-cards.mjs` reads it from `src/utils/unlock.ts` into `cards.js`), and keeps the unlock under the site's own `localStorage` key, so on rdeboerdesigns.com a visitor unlocked on the site is unlocked in the demo and the other way round; another tab unlocking is picked up too. Unlocked, the lock pills go, the protected cards' backs load, and the card that was clicked flips into its study (Ryan, 20 October: the password should open the whole binder for anyone he hands it to). Any other password gets a note and a link to the study on rdeboerdesigns.com, where the private password works too; the card nudges in its sleeve and stays put. Until then a protected card's back image and study wait unloaded: the data is in the page, as it is in the site's bundle, since the site's gate is a courtesy gate that withholds nothing. Seasonal content has no intro video, so its back and the top of its study are its overview's opening image, held still. The bench has an Unlocked toggle for held frames of the protected cards.

**The panel** is a mock of the site's fly-out with the same geometry as `.project-panel` and `.project-overview`: 95vw from the right, a 68px header, a 280px rail beside the walkthrough. A reel such as PlayDraft's plays square-cornered on dark, and a cover loop sits in a 10px frame, as on the site. It carries each open study's own media where the live overview puts it, laid out by the same rules as `ProjectOverview`: a decision's capture beside its copy, a row of live variants under it, portrait captures capped at 620px tall, PlayDraft's wide boards and its countdown loop, Bolus Binder's crops, Heatherwood's board in a phone you scroll, and the outcome images. That is 25 images and loops across the five open studies, all the live overview shows. Loops play only while they are in view, and never under reduced motion. On phones the pocket leads, centred, then the video or the intro, then the rail.

**The bench panel** picks a card, opens and closes it, switches the panel's top, plays the sequence four times slower, holds five frames of the open timeline for matched stills, and toggles the proofs: holo themes or plain silver, hide the sleeves, explode the layers, fix the light, force reduced motion. Keys 1 to 8 hold the frames, the arrow keys scrub a held frame (Shift for bigger steps), and Esc lets go.

## What holds

1. Twelve real cards read as one collection, and every hero is legible at slot size, about 320px wide on desktop.
2. One light: the reflection, the rainbow and the dimming all come from one lamp, and moving it moves the highlight across the page rather than six copies of it.
3. The card is the transition. The thing you clicked is the thing at the top of the study, in a sleeve of its own; nothing cross-fades, and the handoff lands on the same pixels.
4. The binder is also the navigation: the spine files the work by discipline on desktop, and the same tabs run along the cover on smaller screens.
5. The materials did not change with the art. Sleeve, foil and light still answer over every hero, light or dark.
6. Locked cards leak nothing a visitor could not already see. Their backs and panels are the same data the site's bundle carries behind the same courtesy gate, opened by the same shared password, and nothing in them loads until then.

## Notes for Opus

- The cards are data, not markup. `cards.js` is the content model this page needs; a real `cardHero` field on each project, with alt text and a lock-aware public flag, would replace the manifest lookup. Public cards carry `back.intro` (video, poster and the poster's time in the video, size, crop focus, reel or preview) and `panel` (the fly-out's opening copy, including the problem lines). Every card carries its `groups` for the spine.
- The lamp is two custom properties (`--kx`, `--ky`); the reflection is two numbers in the script. The band, prism and gloss sheets are drawn once and moved with `translate3d`, so a moving light only composites: 60fps with the worst frame at 19ms on an M4. An earlier version of this pass repainted all six cards on every step of the light and ran at 29fps with 83ms frames. Keep anything that follows the light off the cards.
- `layout()` (on a ResizeObserver) measures the binder (`--bw`, `--bh`) and each pocket's offset (`--px`, `--py`), and rebuilds the SVGs that depend on the layout only when it changes: the film's noise field (`--blanket`), the prism's mask (one even-odd path of the card faces less their art windows), the sleeves' mask for the glare and the band, and the page's mask (the binder less the spine) for the lamp's dimming and the hand.
- The spine is one `<nav>`: a column at 1180px and up, a strip across the cover below that. Its tabs are `aria-pressed` buttons. Filtering pages through the filtered list and fills short pages with empty pockets; the light's mask skips them.
- The sequence is the Web Animations API, one timeline per direction with every element on the same clock, so held frames are `currentTime` on paused animations and slow motion is one multiplier. At the end the animated state is committed to classes with transitions off, then the animations are cancelled.
- Stacking: pockets, the grid and the binder make no stacking context, so cards, sleeves, the light sheets, the scrim and the panel share the root. Seated: card 2, sleeves 3, the light 6 to 10. A raised card is 4 under its own sleeve at 5, and its shadow rises to 3 to fall on the neighbouring plastic. Once clear of the page a card is 50, over the panel (46) and the scrim (44). The panel's pocket and its plastic live inside the panel, so from the moment the card tucks in, a copy of that plastic (`.fo-plastic`, 51) lies over it, and the card that flew in stays the visible card to the end; only then does a copy of the card in the panel's pocket take over. Inside the panel the pocket (3) sits over the video (2) and the reel's insert (1). Anything that adds a transform, filter or opacity to the binder breaks the flight.
- Card turns swap their faces by `visibility` at the moments they are edge-on, rather than trusting `backface-visibility`: WebKit drew the hidden back face, mirrored, through the front of a card that had turned all the way round. The insert's creases can't do the same. In real Safari (iPadOS 18.6 in the Simulator), a face hidden by a `visibility` animation stayed on screen in 3D, and with `backface-visibility: hidden` as well, Safari culled the second and third panels outright once the third began to turn, so the reel vanished mid-unfold. So the fold's faces carry no `backface-visibility`, and each face and its shade step their opacity at the edge-on moment (`crease()`). The insert as a whole still shows and hides by `visibility`, because an opacity animation on a `preserve-3d` container flattens its 3D in WebKit. Headless WebKit draws the panels flat, so check the fold in the Simulator, not Playwright's WebKit.
- `openStudy()` holds the sequence on its first frame for two animation frames before playing it, so a slow first paint of the panel (Safari's especially) delays the start instead of skipping into it.
- An intro's poster is a frame from the middle of its video, not its first (PlayDraft's video opens dark), and the fold is cut from the poster. `build-cards.mjs` records where each poster sits (`posterAt`, found by comparing every frame with the poster), and `cueVideo()` seeks there once the metadata is in, so the video takes over from the flat insert on the same frame. The seek needs a server that answers Range requests, as the live site's nginx does.
- `[hidden]` is `display: none !important`: the lock dialog's actions are a flex row, and `display: flex` beat the attribute, so a locked card showed its Unlock button twice. The Artifact publisher's reset had hidden it.
- The copy of the card in the panel is laid out at the binder card's size, with the binder card's place in the sheet of foil (`--ox`, `--oy`, `--BW`, `--BH`, `--blanket`) and its `--cw`, then scaled to the pocket. That is why it lands on the same pixels; laid out at the pocket's size, its px-sized foil pattern would not match.
- In the app, the panel would be the real `ProjectPanel` dialog. The card has to fly in a layer above it (a portal at the top of the dialog, or the dialog's own top layer), and the panel would render its own pocket and card.
- While a study is open the header, binder, pager, bench and notes are `inert` and the page stops scrolling; while it opens, the panel's own scroll holds still too, so the card lands where it should. Held frames skip both, so a reviewer can keep using the bench.
- The shared page sets `window.BINDER_SHARE`, which turns off the bench's frame keys. `share-build.mjs` (parked) copied each open study's media beside the page, at most 2000px across, and turns an opaque PNG over 600KB into a JPEG; it reads the alpha with Pillow when it is there, because screenshots often carry an alpha channel that is opaque throughout.
- A page turn copies the grid into `.binder__turn` (z 5, under the light sheets at 6 to 10), in two `preserve-3d` segments under a perspective of five page widths. Going back, `.turn__under` (the old page, flat) needs its own z-index: without it its cards' z-indexes escape it and paint over the incoming sheet. `turnBy()` lays the next page out under the copy; going back, it renders the page before just long enough to copy it. `finishTurn()` lands any turn at once, and every path that changes the page (`showPage`, the pick, a filter, a held frame, opening a study) calls it first. The turn runs the whole 180°, and a turn back starts from the sheet flat on the cover. The pages turned lie in `.binder__landed` (z 2, under the rings at 6), one flat sheet each from `syncLanded()`, which `setPage()` and `finishTurn()` keep in step with the page count; the first frame of a turn back takes the top sheet's place, and below desktop `--turn-clip` cuts both layers at the binder's edge. `window.binder.holdTurn(step, at)`, `seekTurn(at)` and `endTurn()` hold frames for the capture.
- A card's back face is `visibility: hidden` at rest: headless WebKit drew the backs of the public cards through their fronts on the page itself, `backface-visibility` or not. Turns show it through `swapAt` as before.
- On a phone (700px and under) `panelMode()` turns the beside layout into the video layout for the panel's markup and held poses, `filedAsChip()` adds the chip, and `foldOpen()`/`foldClose()` run the fold on `PLAN.fold` (`FOLD_MS`). `foldGeometry()` cuts the gate from the frame's box: the middle panel is the card's back, flush left, so on a phone the card back's art is anchored left too (`object-position: 0%`); the markup's two flaps are the two panels to its right, each hinged on its left edge, and reuse `crease()` a pixel behind the panel before them. Playwright's WebKit draws a folded panel's card stock over the landed card; real Safari (the iPad simulator) and Chromium keep it behind, and the old centred fold showed the same in Playwright's WebKit. The open's first keyframe is the card's live transform (`fromTf`), so a tilted card leaves without a jump; the glare's hole is cut from the card's real box, tilt included.
- The swipe is pointer events on `.pockets` (touch only; `touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` keeps vertical scrolling and the cover's scrolling tab strip). It calls `turnBy(step, 0)`, the held turn the capture uses, scrubs it with `seekTurn()`, and on release plays it on (`playbackRate` 1) or back (-1.5); a turn falling back sets `back` on the leaf, so `finishTurn()`, whoever calls it, lands it on the page it left. A drag back starts in the left 35% of the screen (`BACK_ZONE`), the spine and the cards nearest it, never less than the spine and 14px into the cards; it was the spine alone until Ryan asked for a zone a thumb can find (21 October). A drag forward starts anywhere on the cards. Each touch is decided once, over its first 10px (`SWIPE_DECIDE`): within 45° of horizontal (`SWIPE_ANGLE`) it is a page turn and a non-passive touchmove cancels the browser's own panning for the rest of the touch, so the page never scrolls under a turning sheet; steeper, it is a scroll and the binder lets it go. 45° is where the browser itself hands a pan-y drag to scrolling, so no drag does neither. A turn completes past 30% of the edge's travel, or a 96px sideways drag (`SWIPE_GO_PX`, for wide phones where an angled swipe covers less of the page), or a flick. The finger is followed from touchmove or pointermove, whichever comes first: Safari on iOS 18 sent its first pointermove only after about 90px. Tuned 21 October in Chromium (320–430 wide, -70° to 70°) and in Safari on iOS 26 and iOS 18 simulators. The lock dialog acts on a single tap (`touch-action: manipulation`) and holds the page still while open.
- The rings' glint is two custom properties set in `applyLight()` on `.rings` and each `.ring`, small subtrees, so the moving light still never restyles the cards.
- `buildMasks()` draws the light's three masks from what `layout()` measured (faces, art windows, pockets, the page past the spine) and cuts the raised cards' holes; a MutationObserver on the pockets' classes calls it, since every way a card is raised or let down goes through `pocket--raised`. The geometry is unrounded and the masks are drawn at the binder's exact size, with the glare half a pixel inside each sleeve's welded edge: rounded to whole pixels, the mask edges missed the plastic by up to 0.9px (two device pixels on a phone), and the passing glare drew a hairline along a sleeve or left a bare one inside it, the line Ryan saw on 20 October. While a card slides, the observer redraws the masks each frame from where the card is (its computed transform), so the hole follows it; it used to be cut at once where the card would end up and kept until 460ms after it sank, which left a bare strip with a ruled edge at the foot of the pocket above. A mask that comes out the same is not set again. The other line that showed now and then was on the turning sheet: its two 3D segments met edge to edge at the crease, and the antialiasing between two layers left a hairline down the page at every angle (the Fable review of 20 October found it). The free-edge segment now starts 1.5px before the fold and shows that much more of the page (`SEG_OVERLAP` in `segmentOf()`), so it lies over the hinge segment's edge.
- The homepage mock is `stage--open` plus `html.binder-open` (the hero stand-in). `layout()` sets `--spine-w` and `--index-w` in px from the content column, so the cover, the flat spine (`--hinge-w`) and the index stay put at any desktop width. `share-build.mjs --open` (parked) wrote it to `share-open/`, git-ignored like `share/`, and it was published as its own page.
- The unlock is the site's: `build-cards.mjs` lifts the shared password's hash and the two storage keys from `src/utils/unlock.ts` into `cards.js` (`window.BINDER_UNLOCK`), and the page checks and remembers it the same way (`setUnlocked`, `shut(c)`). If the password or the keys change, rebuild the cards. In the app the binder would call `verifyPassword` and `isUnlocked` directly.
- `--cw` is set once on the grid from the first visible pocket, and every card scales its type from it.

## Not done here

No access enforcement beyond the site's own courtesy gate (the shared password; the private one works only on the site), no Author/Publisher mode work, and no integration into `CaseStudyPlayground` or the real `ProjectPanel`. The Opus pass covers access and integration on its own branch.

**Lighter hover and click (21 October).** Traced in Chromium (paint and raster, not frame timing, which headless drops even at rest), the cost of the hover was repainting, not script:
- The rings' plate glint was a gradient placed at `var(--ly)`, so every move of the lamp repainted the whole page, about 30 times a second at rest. It is now its own element moved by a transform, the rings are a layer, and a ring's glow is only re-set when it changes by 1/40.
- A rising or sinking card re-cut the glare masks every frame; they re-cut in 3px steps, rounded outward, so the hole is never smaller than the card.
- The card's resting and raised shadows are two drawn layers (`.card__shadow::before` and `::after`) crossfaded by opacity; the raise no longer animates `filter` or `box-shadow`, and the lift and drop scale and fade the drawn shadow instead of animating its blur.
- The held card's foil is measured when the pointer arrives, moved at most once a frame in half-pixel steps, directly on its two layers and without a transition while the hand is on it (it blends with the card, so each move repaints the card).
Measured on the same moves, before and after: at rest 296 → 22ms of raster per 2s; a rise 544 → 326ms; holding and moving over a card 1273 → 292ms; a sweep across six cards 2844 → 1261ms. The glare is at 70% of its 70% (0.49) and the pointer's shadow at 70% of its size and darkness (Ryan, 21 October).

**The site's own panel (21 October).** On the homepage a study opens in the site's real ProjectPanel, not the binder's mock: the binder clicks a real `/work/<slug>/` link on the card's behalf (`window.__binderNavigate`, with `data-from-binder`), so the site's password prompt, address, Back, back-arrow strip and return all work. `usePanelNavigation` carries `fromBinder` in the panel's state; `ProjectOverview` then renders `BinderTop` first in the article, and the binder builds its top there (`window.binderHome.mountTop`: the sleeve, the reel and the video, or the phone's gate) in a shadow root with its own styles. The panel is a modal dialog in the browser's top layer, so the card (and the plastic over it) fly as popovers laid over their pockets; the card joins the top layer the moment the panel opens (`portfolio:reading-panel`), before the study, loaded on demand, has mounted its top. Closing, `ProjectPanel` asks first (`portfolio:panel-closing`, a promise): the reel folds and the card leaves its sleeve, then the panel slides away as the card flies home. Back, Forward and links leave the binder to reset or show the study already open.

**The lamp on the compositor (21 October).** Three audits (runtime, load, paint) agreed the largest cost on the homepage was the lamp's sway: a rAF loop writing every sheet about 30 times a second, forever, even off screen. The sheets now ride wrappers animated by looping Web Animations of `transform` (one across, one down: an animated `translate` ran on the main thread), the pointer's lean is a CSS transition on an outer wrapper, and the steel's glints follow the same sway as compositor animations; the sway pauses off screen, in a hidden tab, while a card is out or a page turns. At rest with the binder in view the main thread went from about 270 ms to 2 ms per 3 s and from 180 frames to none; scrolled away, to nothing. The pointer's shadow sheet leaves the tree once it has faded. For the homepage the card art is 800px WebP (5.3 MB to 640 KB) and the card backs load when a card is raised or opened, or once the page is idle.
