What this page claims

  • Every card is real copy. Titles, rows, years and categories come from the project data through a build script. Nothing on a card is typed by hand.
  • The heroes sit under the materials. Each one is a plain static image in the art window. Sleeve gloss, foil and the light path stay separate layers and answer the pointer.
  • Locked cards stop at the gate. The seven professional studies carry a lock and have no back in this page at all. Selecting one opens a password stand-in, and the card stays in its sleeve.
  • Public cards keep the whole sequence. Pull, turn to a back built from the study's own copy and a real capture, then return to the same slot.
  • Pages follow the device. Six cards on desktop, four on a tablet and three on a phone, in the published work order.

What still needs a decision

  1. The heroes are private drafts. They are AI-generated, and small text, charts and logos can differ from the real screens. Publishing them is Ryan's call.
  2. Six of the seven locked heroes show Tire Rack branding. The site anonymizes the client while a study is locked. Approve the branding on the public front, or give those cards neutral covers.
  3. Values are uneven. Real metrics replace the reference's invented numbers, so long values wrap smaller.
  4. Locked backs wait for real access control. That is the Opus pass, running separately.
  5. Paging is a plain pager. How a visitor moves through the collection is still open.
  6. Foil is pastel and edge-on is a painted edge. Both are unchanged from the first pass.
