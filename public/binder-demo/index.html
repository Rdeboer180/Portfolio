    What this page claims

    • One light for the whole page. A lamp hangs over the middle of the binder, and sheets lie over every pocket at once: one dims the page away from the lamp, and three slide with its reflection, a band of light, the rainbow the foil throws round it and the glare on the plastic. The rainbow falls only on the cards' foil, never on the art. Each card's own film and glitter are laid across the binder, so neighbours continue one sheet of foil.
    • The pointer tilts the page. The reflection slides the opposite way, so it crosses the binder card by card and never sits on the card you are pointing at. Left alone, the page sways and the reflection drifts round every card.
    • Hover lifts a card a tenth of the way out. It rises 10% from behind the plastic, so its top clears the mouth while the rest stays glossed.
    • The card you click is the card at the top of the study. It turns once on its way to the panel, its back (the intro video's first frame) flashing past, and tucks into the panel's own sleeve under its plastic. Beside the video, the reel then draws out from behind it. Close plays it in reverse.
    • The spine is the work's index. On desktop the binder's spine lists All work, Product Design, System Design and AEM, as the site files them; a short page keeps its empty sleeves. Below desktop the same tabs run along the cover's top edge.
    • Every word is real copy. The cards and the panel come from the project data through a build script; the panel uses the same fields as the site's fly-out.
    • Locked cards stop at the gate. They lift on hover and carry a lock until the site's shared password opens them, as on the site; the unlock is remembered where the site remembers it, and a locked card's back and study wait unloaded until then.

    What still needs a decision

    1. The heroes are private drafts. They are AI-generated, and small text, charts and logos can differ from the real screens. Publishing them is Ryan's call.
    2. Six of the seven locked heroes show Tire Rack branding. The site anonymizes the client while a study is locked. Approve the branding on the public front, or give those cards neutral covers.
    3. The holo themes step outside the one-accent rule. Twelve hues and a rainbow finish live inside the binder, where the rest of the site keeps Signal Orange as its one accent.
    4. The reflection moves against the pointer, so the card you point at is never in the glare. Following the pointer instead is a one-line change.
    5. The open takes 1.5 seconds, against the site panel's 250ms. It is a moment rather than navigation, but it may want to be shorter once seen.
    6. The panel's top is the card beside the video (Ryan, 20 October). The reel gives up about a third of the column's width for it; the intro version stays on the bench for comparison.
    7. What the spine carries. Here it indexes the work by discipline. The reference put the site's own nav there: Work, About, Notes, Contact.
    8. On phones the card's sleeve leads the panel so the card has somewhere visible to land. The site's fly-out puts the rail first.
    9. The panel here is a mock. In the app, the card would fly above the real dialog and the dialog would render its own sleeve and card; that is integration work for the Opus pass.
    10. Locked studies never transition until real access control exists.
