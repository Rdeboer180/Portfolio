The problem I found

When the agent changes the basket, who sees it?

In conversational shopping, decisions move into a conversation, and the guest can't see how the agent made them. When an agent builds a basket of several products, swaps an item or hands off to pickup, the guest is trusting choices made on their behalf, and the store team inherits those choices. A journey map shows the steps. It can't show what the guest sees when the agent changes something, or what the team member sees when that change lands on their pick list.

How I found it

The team's framing, a problem I've solved before, and the map

  • The team's own framing. Its public posts say the guest's journey increasingly starts with a conversation, and list Prototypes > slide decks among the traits it hires for. Target has also announced a shopping app inside ChatGPT that builds baskets of several products (November 2025) and a partnership with Google (January 2026).
  • I've designed this trust problem before. In WheelRack, Tire Rack's dealer platform, when a dealer picks a finish their size isn't made in, the system changes the order for them and has to say so. The static frame showed the result, not the change. I rebuilt it in code: the rule became testable (diameter, then width, then offset), the change stays visible, Undo is offered, and with reduced motion a static Changed tag stays.
  • Mapping it as a blueprint showed the backstage cost. A substitution the guest never saw becomes a pickup the team member has to explain.

    The handoff

    What engineering gets with this prototype

    The prototype is the spec. Each numbered pin in a working moment matches a note here, and the checks run against the same state engine the blueprint uses.

    Dev notes

    1. 1

      The agent never substitutes outside the guest's rule

      A swap applies on its own only when it's the same type and within the guest's price limit. Anything else is proposed and waits for the guest. With “Ask me before any swap”, nothing changes without an answer. The rule is checked in code, outside the model.

      Owned by: swapStatus() and RULES; in production, the order service, not the agent's prompt
    2. 2

      Every change stays visible and undoable until picking starts

      A swapped item keeps its Changed tag, its reason and the price difference in the chat, the cart and the pick list. Undo shows until the store starts picking. After that the change stays visible without Undo, and any further change goes through the store.

      Owned by: canUndo() and pick.picking
    3. 3

      Prices are the chosen store's, with an as-of time

      Every price names its store and the time it was read. If a price differs from the one in the chat, the line shows both and the total keeps the old price until the guest says OK. Moving to a new store re-quotes every line.

      Owned by: ITEMS[].price[store], line.was and line.needsOk
    4. 4

      The team member sees the guest's rule, not just the item

      The pick list carries the guest's swap rule on every order. A swap line shows what to pick, what it replaces, the rule and who approved it. If the guest doesn't answer within 10 minutes, the line says what the rule allows, so nobody guesses or calls.

      Owned by: pickView().swap.rule, read from the order's swapRule field
    5. 5

      The handoff keeps each item's reason

      The payload from the conversation to the app carries items, reasons, the swap rule, the store and the pickup time. The checklist compares what arrived with what was sent, and anything missing shows as Lost.

      Owned by: carried() and the handoff payload

    Acceptance checks · also agent eval cases

    Run against

      Parity check: does the branch match the prototype?

      • The same swap states and labels: in stock, changed within your rule, needs your OK, outside your rule, removed, found after all.
      • The swap rule travels with the order and shows on the pick list, with or without a swap.
      • Undo shows until picking starts; after that the Changed tag stays without it.
      • Every price carries a store and an as-of time, and a changed price waits for the guest.
      • The handoff payload has items, reasons, swap rule, store and pickup time; a missing field shows as Lost.
      • The same aria-live text on each change, and a static Changed tag under reduced motion.

      Engineering decides: the agent model and what each partner platform allows (what can be sent, shown or confirmed inside someone else's chat); the inventory and pricing services, and how fresh an as-of time can be; the channels for reaching the guest during picking; and how real stores run picking and pickup. I'd ask store teams before treating any of my pick states as right.

      Scope

      What this is, and what I'd ask

      What it isn't

      It isn't Target's experience, and it uses no internal knowledge. It explores public announcements only. Products, prices, stores, times and confidence scores are synthetic, and the page is unbranded.

      What I'd ask the team

      1. Where do guests lose trust today between the conversation and pickup?
      2. When a guest doesn't answer during picking, what do store teams do now, and what would they want the guest to have decided up front?
      3. Which choices should an agent never make for a guest, even inside a rule the guest set?
