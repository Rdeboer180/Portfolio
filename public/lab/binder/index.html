Portfolio binder

My homepage case studies, rebuilt as a binder of collectible cards with Claude agents from 19 to 21 October. The prompts below are where I steered it.

See it on rdeboerdesigns.com ↗

What I did

  • Set the direction: one lamp over the whole binder, the card's back as the study's intro video, and no morphing on phones.
  • Set the bar for the rings, the spine, and the page turn, and gave numbers when something felt off. Hover lift 10%, glare 70%.
  • Tested it myself on a live demo page and found two bugs the headless tests missed.
  • Went against the agents' written advice twice. The sleeve layout shipped. The desk scene didn't.
  • Shipped it to my homepage on 21 October.
  • After launch, reverted an agent's scroll fix that passed its own test, then shipped one that keeps scrolling live and keeps the card under the header. The fixes

Tradeoffs

  • The desk scene took an evening, and I cut it after seeing it built.
  • I kept the glare and the grain, and took the speed from the card art instead: 5.3MB down to 640KB.
  • Motion is the point of the binder, so reduced motion gets its own path.

Worth asking about

  • The old card grid was fine. It also looked like something anyone can generate in an afternoon now.
  • The agents wrote the code and ran the measurements. The look, the motion, and what got cut were my calls.
  • The prompts that worked named a real object or gave a number.
  • The homepage got faster. Card art went from 5.3MB to 640KB, and desktop first paint from 1.52s to 0.87s.
  • Reduced motion opens the study in one step, and Escape puts focus back on the card.

Before and after

Before: a header, a testimonial, filter chips, a pager, and white cards with a schematic or a screenshot. After: a binder lying open under the hero. The spine files the work by discipline, hover pulls a card out of its sleeve, and a click carries it into the study.

The card art is AI-generated preview art. Locked cards are employer work, and their studies stay behind the site's password.

  2. 3fc1b2d24 Oct · 22:36FeedbackQuestion

    Using it myself broke it, so I reverted and asked for options

    RD

    Ryan 22:36

    revert to the prior version, this fix broke the scroll to where if i scroll while the animaiton is ongoing the scroll is still fixed to the canvas behind the fly out

    RD

    Ryan 22:36

    what was our other option to explore in regards to fixing the card animation's fixed positioning? can we lock vertical scrolling until the animation ends? can we make it relative while the animation is in progress?

    What changed

    With the panel held, my scroll fell through to the page behind it. Three options came back: lock all scrolling for about a second and a half, hand the card into the panel mid-flight and risk a one-frame jump, or let the card follow the scroll. I picked the one that keeps scrolling live, since that was my complaint both times.

    RD

    Ryan 22:36

    Build the follow-the-scroll fix locally: the flying card picks up the panel's scroll offset, blended in over the flight, so it lands on the sleeve while scrolling stays live. Show me locally before pushing.

    How the follow works

    • Each frame, the card takes on how far the panel has scrolled since takeoff: none as it leaves its pocket, all of it by the time it reaches the sleeve.
    • Progress only moves forward, so it always lands where the sleeve is.
    • It rides on the translate property, so the flight's own keyframes stay untouched.

  3. 1f01eb725 Oct · 00:37Test

    The card flew over the header until it landed

    RD

    Ryan 00:37

    it looks like its z-index is higher than the nav (<header class="project-panel__header">) z-index until the animation finalizes which looks off, please fix then this can go live

    What changed

    The browser's top layer draws above everything, the panel's header included, so raising the header's z-index could never win. Instead, every frame the card is up there (the lift while the study loads, the flight, and the close), it's clipped away wherever it passes under the header, which slides in and out with the panel. The clip lives in the two helpers that move an element into and out of the top layer, so every path through the binder gets it.

    I caught it testing on localhost and on my phone, before it went live. It shipped with the follow-the-scroll fix as one commit.

    Checked on the live site

    • 1440, 1280, 768 and 390 wide, with the panel scrolled mid-flight.
    • The card lands on the sleeve exactly where it does without a scroll.
    • The page behind the panel stays put on desktop and tablet.
    • No frame draws the card over the header.

The agent's check passed because it tested what it was told to test. Using it myself is what failed it.

Revert fast, ask for the options, pick the one that matches the complaint, and test on a real device before it goes live.
