Experience Systems Lab · concept, not a Tire Rack page

Side by side at the counter

The same 30-tire snow test, turned into something an advisor can show a customer: the tire on the truck now next to the one we'd put on. Three ways to show it. Every number is from Tire Rack's published test. The quote is the shop's own number.

S1

Two trucks, one road

Both trucks brake at the same line. The green strip is the room the upgrade gives back. Drawn to scale, one truck is 18 ft.

S2

Head to head

A scorecard that fits on an estimate or a text message. Each row says who wins and by how much, with the losing number greyed out, not hidden.

S3

Walk them through it

One point per screen for a tablet turned toward the customer. The advisor taps through and ends on the price next to what it buys.

Rules every option follows

  • Both tires are shown new, as tested. A worn set on the truck today stops longer still, so the real gap is bigger. The page says so instead of guessing a number.
  • Losses are shown, not hidden. If the upgrade is worse at something, it reads in red with the same weight as a win.
  • Under 2 ft reads "about the same". Gaps of 7 ft and up also read in truck lengths (the test truck, a Tacoma, is about 18 ft).
  • No price is invented. The shop types its own quote, and the page pairs it with what it buys.

Source: Tire Rack, 30 truck tires on snow and ice, Oct 12, 2026. Owner ratings from Tire Rack's public survey, read Nov 11, 2026. A design concept by Ryan DeBoer, not affiliated with Tire Rack.
